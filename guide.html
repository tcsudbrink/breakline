Tutorials

How to use Breakline

A short walk-through for every feature. Each one says what it's for, then the taps to do it. Button names are in boxes just like they look in the app.

Everything below works the same on a phone, tablet or computer.

Start here

Getting set up

Two minutes to set your home port and put Breakline on your phone's home screen.

  1. Go to breaklinefishing.com/app and create your account. Pick your home port on the sign-up form.
  2. Your home port sits at the top left of the chart. Range, bearing, run limits and Where to fish are all measured from it, so change it whenever you trailer somewhere else.
  3. Use Jump to… beside it to fly to any fishing area: the canyons, the Gulf Stream off Hatteras, the Bahamas, Cabo, Costa Rica and more.
  4. Pick a chart from the bar down the left side: Temp, Hourly, Color, Altimetry and so on. One chart shows at a time, like the paid chart services.
  5. Put it on your phone: on iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app. It then opens full screen like an app.

Tip: your spots, home port and settings are saved to your account, so they follow you to every phone and computer you sign in on.

Reading any spot

Tap anywhere on the water and Breakline tells you everything it knows about that spot, in plain English.

  1. Tap the chart. A box opens with the position, wind and seas right now, and every reading that's loaded: water temp, color, altimetry, current, depth and more.
  2. Tap the small i next to any reading to see what it means and what to expect there (for example, "you are on a temperature break, fish the warm side").
  3. Best times here shows the solunar periods, sunrise, sunset and tides for that spot. Weather forecast shows wind and seas by the hour.
  4. Type a name and tap Save to keep it as a spot.
  5. Find this water again copies that spot's temp, color, depth and break into Find your water, so you can see where that same water is now or get an alert when it comes back (see Water alerts).

Tip: on a computer, just move the mouse: the box at the bottom left shows the reading under the pointer without tapping.

The color bar and the i button

The bar at the bottom right is the key to the chart, and it lets you stretch the colors so small breaks stand out.

  1. Tap the round i beside the chart name to open a short guide: what you're looking at, what's best for fishing, and the limits of the data.
  2. Drag the two white handles toward each other to spend all the colors on a narrow range. For example, set water temp to 72-78°F and a 1-degree break turns into a sharp color change.
  3. Tap Auto to fit the colors back to whatever is on screen.
  4. The right side of the bar says how old the data is, and says saved copy when NOAA isn't answering and you're on Breakline's own backup.

Dates, passes and forecasts

Look back at older charts, pick a single satellite pass, or look ahead at the forecast.

  1. Use ◀ and ▶ at the top to step back and forward a day (an hour on Hourly). Tap the date to pick any day.
  2. Tap Charts ▾ to see every satellite pass and product for this area as thumbnails. Clouds show as blank, so you can pick the clearest look in one tap. Switch between Water temp, Hourly temp and Chlorophyll along the top.
  3. On Currents, Temp and Hourly, keep tapping ▶ past today to step into the forecast in 6-hour jumps (up to 7 days for currents, 3 days for temp).
  4. Latest takes you back to now.

Chart layers

Water temp

The first chart most fishermen check: temperature breaks are where bait collects and predators hunt.

  1. Tap Temp. Breaks show as places where the colors bunch up tight.
  2. Open Charts ▾ to choose the source. Single passes (morning, afternoon, night) are the sharpest but have cloud holes. The cloud-free blends fill the holes but smooth out small breaks. The 1-day and 3-day composites keep the newest clear look for every spot.
  3. Drag the color bar handles around the temps your species likes so the breaks pop.
  4. Turn on Temperature breaks in Layers to have Breakline outline them for you. The sensitivity slider adds more or fewer.

Tip: work the warm side of a break, especially where it crosses a canyon head or the 100 fathom curve.

Hourly temp

Fresh temps from the GOES-19 weather satellite, which looks at the whole coast every hour.

  1. Tap Hourly. Each spot shows the newest clear look from the last 24 hours.
  2. Step through the hours with ◀ ▶ to watch a break or eddy edge move, then set up ahead of it.
  3. Check it the morning you go: a break can move several miles overnight in the Gulf Stream.

Tip: it's coarser than the polar passes (about 2 km), so use Hourly to see where things are now and Temp to see the fine detail.

Color (chlorophyll)

Water color from space: blue is clean and low on food, green is full of plankton and bait. The line between them is one of the best signs offshore.

  1. Tap Color. Purple and blue is clean blue water, green and yellow is plankton-rich, orange and red is dirty coastal water.
  2. Look for color edges: blue meeting green within a mile or two.
  3. Use Charts ▾ to pick a single clear pass, or the gap-free products when it's cloudy. Check the date on the color bar.

Tip: for billfish and tuna, fish the blue side of the edge, around 0.1-0.3 mg/m³. A color edge that lines up with a temp break is the best of both.

Altimetry

Satellite radar measures how high the sea surface sits. That shows eddies and the Gulf Stream even under clouds.

  1. Tap Altimetry. Red (+) is high water: a warm eddy or the Stream, turning clockwise. Blue (-) is low water: a cold eddy, turning counterclockwise.
  2. Turn on Altimetry rings (eddies) in Layers to outline the eddies on any chart, including Temp and Color.
  3. Fish the edges, where the colors bunch up: that's where the current runs fastest.

Tip: on a cloudy week when Temp is blank, Altimetry still tells you where the warm water is.

Currents

How fast the water is moving and which way, for planning your drift, your spread, and which way a break will move.

  1. Tap Currents. Colors are speed in knots; arrows show direction.
  2. Pick a source in the bar at the top: Satellite (measured, with HF radar near the coast), HYCOM or RTOFS (ocean models that cover everywhere).
  3. Tap ▶ past today to see the current forecast up to a week out.
  4. Turn on Currents in Layers to put the arrows on top of any other chart.

Tip: when the two models disagree about where the Stream edge is, and HF radar covers your area, trust the radar.

Salinity

How salty the water is. A sharp change in saltiness marks the edge between two bodies of water, even when the temps have evened out.

  1. Tap Salinity. Blue is salty open-ocean water (Gulf Stream, 36 and up); brown is fresher shelf and river water.
  2. Use the Depth menu at the top to look below the surface, down to about 1,000 ft.
  3. Look for salinity edges and river plumes (the Mississippi, the Chesapeake). Weed, bait and mahi line up along plume edges.

Tip: the surface reading is coarse and can't see close to land. Use it for the big picture, then fine-tune on Temp and Color.

Mixed layer

How deep the warm surface layer goes before the water turns cold. It tells you how deep the fish can be.

  1. Tap Mix layer. Light colors are shallow, dark colors are deep.
  2. Shallow (under about 100-150 ft): bait and fish are pinned near the surface. Good for trolling and topwater.
  3. Deep (200 ft and more): fish can spread out and go down. Run planers, downriggers or deep divers, or drop.

Tip: NOAA doesn't compute it over most of the continental shelf, and not at all in the Pacific, so blank areas are normal.

Oxygen

How much oxygen is dissolved in the water, from the Copernicus ocean model. Tuna and billfish burn a lot of it, so low-oxygen water at depth pushes them up.

  1. Tap Oxygen, then use the Depth menu at the top. The surface is almost always fine; the deeper levels are where this chart earns its keep.
  2. Set the depth to 150-300 ft. Orange and red there means low oxygen close to the surface: fish get squeezed into the top layer and are easier to raise on the troll.
  3. Blue at depth means plenty of oxygen, so fish can spread out and go deep.

Tip: this matters most in the eastern Pacific (Costa Rica, Panama, Guatemala, Chiapas, Cabo). Off the U.S. East Coast it barely changes.

Fronts (FSLE)

Breakline works out from the last several days of currents where water is being pushed together into lines. Weed, debris and bait collect on those lines.

  1. Tap Fronts. The bright ridges are convergence lines.
  2. Compare with Temp or Color: a bright ridge that lines up with a break is a front that's real and being fed.
  3. Use it on cloudy days. It comes from currents, not pictures, so clouds don't matter.

Tip: it's coarse (about 15 nm). Use it to choose which area to run to, then find the exact edge on Temp or Color.

Weather

Wind and wave forecasts across the whole run, not just at the beach.

  1. Tap Weather, then Wind or Waves at the top.
  2. Step ahead with ▶ to find your weather window.
  3. For a single spot, tap it and choose Weather forecast for an hour-by-hour table.

Tip: watch for wind blowing against the current, like a northeaster on the Gulf Stream: it stands the seas up. Always check the NOAA marine forecast before you go.

Bottom

Shaded bottom relief so canyons, ledges and humps stand out.

  1. Tap Bottom for the full relief chart, or turn on Bottom relief shading and Depth lines in Layers to put them under any chart.
  2. Zoom in close and turn on Drop-offs (orange is steep bottom) and the NOAA nautical chart for channels, buoys and soundings.

Overlays

Stack extra information on top of whatever chart you're on.

  1. Tap Layers on the left.
  2. Tick what you want: temperature breaks, isotherms every 1°F, eddy rings, currents, depth lines, drop-offs, fishing spots and reefs, wrecks and fish havens, place names, live buoys and inlets.
  3. Under Grid, pick lat/long, Loran TDs, both or none.

Finding fish

Where to fish

Breakline scores every spot inside your run for the species you pick, and marks the best ones.

  1. Tap Where to fish on the left.
  2. Pick your species. The list follows your home port (Atlantic, Gulf or Pacific species, plus inshore fish like flounder, stripers, redfish and snook).
  3. Set Max run to how far you'll go, and a depth range if you want one.
  4. The chart turns into a heat map (yellow is best) with up to 8 numbered yellow circles. The list says why each one scored well and how far it is from your port.
  5. Inshore species pick from inlets, jetties, piers, reefs, shoals and drop-offs on screen, with the tides at the nearest station for each one.

Tip: log your catches with Track my day. After three or more of a species, the scoring tunes itself to the water you actually caught them in.

The numbered circles

Yellow circles are Where to fish hot spots. Blue circles are Find your water matches. Tap any of them to see why it's worth the run, and save it.

  1. Tap a numbered circle on the chart (or tap it in the list).
  2. The box opens with Why go here: the score, the temp compared with your species' sweet spot, whether it's on a temp break, a color edge, steep bottom or an eddy edge, the current, the depth, and the range and run time from your port.
  3. What to do gives a plan for that spot, like "troll along the break, warm side first".
  4. Tap Save as spot. It's saved with the reasons, so a week later you still know why you marked it.

Ocean analysis

An automatic read of the water: breaks, color edges, current edges and eddy centers outlined and numbered, with a written run plan.

  1. Tap Analysis and pick your species.
  2. Read the write-up for Right now or Tomorrow.
  3. Tap Copy write-up to text it to your crew.

Find your water

You say what water you want. Breakline marks every patch inside your run that matches all of it.

  1. Tap Find water on the left.
  2. Set From (your port) and Run up to (how many miles you'll go).
  3. Pick Hourly or Blend for the temps. Hourly is fresher but has cloud holes; Blend sees through clouds but is about a day old.
  4. Tick the things that matter and set a range for each: depth, water temp, temp break (on, strong, or away from breaks), color, current speed (from satellite, HYCOM or RTOFS), mixed layer and salinity (a range or on an edge).
  5. Or tap Use species to fill in the temp and depth for your species, or Use my catches to fill them in from the water you've caught fish in.
  6. Tap Find water. Matching water is shaded blue, with numbered blue circles on the biggest patches, biggest first.

Tip: nothing matches? The message shows what share of the water each setting allows on its own. The one with the smallest share is doing the cutting, so widen it or turn it off.

Water alerts

Save the water you caught fish in, and Breakline emails you when that water shows up within reach of your port again.

  1. Tap the spot you were fishing and choose Find this water again. Find your water opens with that spot's temp, color, depth and break filled in. You can also start from any search you built yourself.
  2. Tap 🔔 Save as alert, name it, and set the smallest patch worth hearing about (5 square miles to start).
  3. Breakline re-checks it every time new charts arrive, up to every 10 minutes. When the water shows up, you get an email with how much there is, where, and a button that opens the chart right on it.
  4. Your alerts are listed under Your water alerts, showing whether each is matching now. Use Show, Pause or Delete.

Tip: while the water stays, you get at most one email a day, so a big patch won't flood your inbox.

Best times and tides

When to be there: solunar periods, sunrise and sunset, and the tide turns.

  1. Tap any spot and choose Best times here, or look under Best times in the Where to fish panel.
  2. Major and minor solunar periods are marked, and the windows where they line up with a tide change or sunrise and sunset get a ★.
  3. Inshore picks list the tides at the nearest NOAA station and when to fish them.

On the boat

Track my day

Record your run, then answer a few quick questions at the dock. Your catches teach Where to fish what water you actually catch fish in.

  1. Leaving the inlet, tap Trip on the left, then Start tracking. Your track draws on the chart.
  2. Keep Breakline open and the phone plugged in. Phones pause web GPS when the screen locks, so leave Keep the screen on while tracking ticked.
  3. Caught one? Tap Log a catch here to drop it right where the boat is.
  4. At the end of the day, tap Stop & review my day. Breakline finds every stop and hard turn and asks why (birds, weed line, a temp break, marking fish on the sounder…) and what you caught there.
  5. Use Add a catch on the map for anything you missed, then Save my day.

Tip: forgot to start tracking? You can still tap Add a catch on the map and place catches by hand. They count just the same.

Spots and GPX

Save numbers, keep them on every device, and send them to your chartplotter.

  1. Tap the chart, type a name, and tap Save. Saved spots show as markers with their names.
  2. Tap Spots on the left to see your list with lat/long, Loran TDs, water temp and depth when you saved it. Tap a name to fly there.
  3. Tap Export GPX to download every spot as a GPX file you can load into most chartplotters (Garmin, Simrad, Lowrance, Raymarine) or Navionics.

Loran TDs

Still have numbers in an old logbook? Breakline reads and writes Loran TDs.

  1. Open Layers and set Grid to Loran TDs or Both, then pick your TD pair.
  2. Under Go to Loran TDs, enter both numbers and tap Go.
  3. If your plotter reads a known spot a little differently, open Calibrate TDs and enter the difference. Every spot after that is corrected.

Measuring a run

Distance, bearing and run time for a trip with several legs.

  1. Tap the ruler button at the top right of the chart.
  2. Tap the chart to drop points. Each leg shows its distance and bearing, and the bar shows the total and about how long it takes at 25 knots.
  3. Undo removes the last point; Done finishes.

Offshore, no signal

Take the charts with you when the phone loses service past the sea buoy.

  1. At the dock, on Wi-Fi or good signal, tap Spots, then Download for offshore.
  2. Breakline saves the last 48 hours of every chart for your home port's area: temp, hourly scans, color, altimetry, currents, salinity and mixed layer.
  3. Offshore, open Breakline as usual. The saved charts load, and Find your water works too.

Tip: weather, tides and HF radar need a signal. Check them before you leave.

When NOAA is down

There's nothing to do: Breakline keeps its own copy of every chart.

  1. Breakline checks NOAA for new charts every 10 minutes and saves a copy.
  2. If NOAA's servers stop answering, the app switches to the saved copy on its own, and the color bar says saved copy.
